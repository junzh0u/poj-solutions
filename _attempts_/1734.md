## Problem

Minimum-weight simple cycle (≥3 distinct vertices) in an undirected weighted graph, N≤100, M≤10000, weights <500; output the cycle's vertices or `No solution.`.

## Algorithm (believed correct)

Floyd minimum-cycle: collapse multi-edges to per-pair minima, then during Floyd–Warshall, before relaxing with vertex `k`, close cycles `i —shortest(i,j)— j — k — i` for `i<j<k` via direct edges `g[j][k]`, `g[k][i]`. The `i..j` path uses only vertices `<k`, so the cycle is simple.

**Pitfall found and fixed pre-submission** (independently, then confirmed on the discuss board with a concrete case): the best cycle's path must be reconstructed and saved *at the moment it becomes best* — reconstructing once at the end from the final `pos[][]` routes through vertices not yet available at that `k` and yields a repeated-vertex cycle.

Validation: brute-force DFS, an independent edge-removal Floyd reference, and an edge-removal Dijkstra reference at full scale — ~9,000 random trials total, zero mismatches; also reproduces the discuss board's community test case exactly.
