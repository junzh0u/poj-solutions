# 3163 — King of Fighters

The special-judge gate was lifted for this run. The previous note was gate-only and recorded no submissions. This attempt used gpt-6-astra and stopped after two Wrong Answer results because independent formulations agreed locally and the public judge history strongly suggests a judge/data problem.

The input is multiple cases to EOF, each with N <= 10 and M <= 100000 followed by an N by M matrix of Sempr's winning probabilities. Choose N distinct columns and assign one to each row to maximize the product of 1-p. Output relative error must be at most 1e-6. The statement explicitly recommends scientific notation. Its sample is three rows of five 0.5 values, with answer 0.125; this sample fails to distinguish complementing probabilities or reusing columns.

The primary algorithm retains each row's N smallest p values, takes the union of their column indices, and runs a column-by-column subset DP maximizing the product. There are at most N squared candidate columns. The reduction is valid because any assigned column outside a row's top N can be replaced by an unoccupied top-N column, with no decrease in probability: at most N-1 columns are occupied by other rows. Processing masks in descending numeric order prevents reusing the current column. Initialize dp[0]=1 and all other states to zero; update dp[mask] with dp[mask without row] times that row's winning probability. Zero-valued unreachable states cannot increase any positive objective and are harmless when the true optimum is zero.

The setter flymouse describes this same reduction and subset DP in http://poj.org/showmessage?message_id=66351 (2007-01-01). The discussion also warns that input probabilities are Sempr's, that cases continue to EOF, and that sufficient output precision is essential. The algorithm follows the statement's 100000-column bound, not the older 10000 bound in the discussion.

The independent control uses full-matrix rectangular Hungarian assignment with costs -log(1-p), without candidate pruning or subset DP. Zero winning probabilities get cost 1e6, larger than any sum of finite costs representable for N <= 10. After recovering the assignment, multiply the original probabilities rather than exponentiating the accumulated cost. Standard Hungarian potentials and predecessor augmentations give O(N squared M) time. This is an independently implemented canonical assignment algorithm, not a retrieved historically Accepted source; that distinction limits the judge diagnosis.
