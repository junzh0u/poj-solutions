# 2096 Collecting Bugs — not accepted

Three submissions, all Wrong Answer. Parked because the judge appears to reject every correct program, not because the recurrence is wrong.

## The problem

Each day finds one bug, uniform over n categories and s subsystems independently. Expected days until every category and every subsystem has been seen at least once.

## The solution

`E[i][j]` is the expected days remaining with i categories and j subsystems already covered, with `E[n][s] = 0` and the answer at `E[0][0]`. From `(i, j)` a day moves to `(i+1, j+1)`, `(i+1, j)`, `(i, j+1)` or stays put, with weights `(n-i)(s-j)`, `(n-i)j`, `i(s-j)`, `ij` over `ns`. The self-loop is removed algebraically rather than iterated — multiply through by `ns` and move the `ij·E[i][j]` term across:

```
E[i][j] = ( ns + (n-i)(s-j)·E[i+1][j+1] + (n-i)j·E[i+1][j] + i(s-j)·E[i][j+1] ) / (ns - ij)
```

`ns - ij` vanishes only at the absorbing state, which is skipped. Filling both indices downward is O(n·s): 0.01s and ~8MB at the maximum 1000×1000.

## Verified three independent ways

- The sample, `1 2` → `3.0000`.
- Monte Carlo over 200k trials.
- An independent forward computation, `E = Σ_t (1 − A(t)·B(t))`, using the exact distribution of distinct coupons after t draws — the two coupon-collector processes are independent, so the finish time is the max of them.
- Analytically, `1 1000` → `7485.4709` = 1000·H₁₀₀₀, and `1000 1000` → `8176.5697`, the commonly cited value.

`double` and `long double` agree to ten decimals, so precision is not in question.
