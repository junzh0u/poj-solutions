# 2164 — Find the Border

The special-judge gate was explicitly lifted for this run. The previous record was gate-only: no actual prior attempt. Three submissions were made with gpt-6-astra, all receiving Wrong Answer. Suggested classification: judge.

The input is a closed polyline of 3 to 100 distinct integer vertices, coordinates from 0 through 100. Its interior includes every bounded face, regardless of winding number. The output is its outer boundary with interior on the left, retaining self-touchings but removing adjacent collinear edges. The sample confirms counterclockwise orientation; a 2005 discussion post suggesting the opposite contradicts the sample. No leading case count is specified; a published solution reads through EOF.

The original implementation enumerates pairwise segment intersections using integer cross products, represents each point by the normalized homogeneous triple (x numerator, y numerator, denominator), and merges equal triples exactly. It sorts each segment's points by its intersection parameter and links consecutive points to form a planar straight-line graph. At the lexicographically smallest vertex it begins along the smallest-angle outgoing edge, then at each vertex takes the counterclockwise successor of the reversed incoming edge. This follows the unbounded face on the right and the interior on the left. Vertices between consecutive pieces of the same original segment are removed. Traversal ends on the starting directed edge, not merely the starting vertex, preserving self-touchings. Eight decimal places are printed. All integer calculations are bounded safely within signed 32-bit arithmetic under the stated constraints.

It reads to EOF and prints through iostream.
