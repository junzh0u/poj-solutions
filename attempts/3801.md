## Statement

POJ 3801 "Crazy Circuits" (Pacific Northwest 2008 regional, I; also HDU 3157).
A circuit board has junctions 1..N plus two special terminals `+` and `-`.
Components are directed edges (positive lead -> negative lead) each with a
required minimum current; current is not "used up" so it just needs to flow
+ -> - through the DAG of components, and each junction freely splits
inflow among its outgoing components in any proportion. The `+` terminal
never appears as a negative lead and `-` never appears as a positive lead.
The circuit is guaranteed acyclic. Find the minimum total current injected
at `+` so every component carries at least its required current, or print
`impossible` if no assignment can satisfy every component simultaneously
(this happens when a junction has forced inflow but no outgoing edge to
carry it away, or forced outflow with no inflow source — see the sample's
own figure, where junction 4 in the second circuit is unreachable from the
negative terminal).

Multiple test cases per file, terminated by a `0 0` line (not to be
processed). N in [0,50], M in [1,200], required current in [1,100].

No ambiguity found in the statement itself.

## Algorithm

This is the standard "minimum flow with lower bounds, no upper bounds" DAG
problem:

1. Number nodes 0=`+`(S), 1..N junctions, N+1=`-`(T).
2. For every component edge (p,n,low): add a residual edge p->n with huge
   capacity (the current above the requirement is unbounded), and account
   the lower bound as node excess: excess[n] += low, excess[p] -= low.
3. Add an artificial completion edge T->S with huge capacity (turns the
   S-T flow problem into a circulation-feasibility problem).
4. Build a super source SS and super sink TT: SS->v with capacity
   excess[v] for every v with positive excess, v->TT with capacity
   -excess[v] for every v with negative excess.
5. Max-flow SS->TT (Dinic). If it doesn't saturate every SS edge (flow !=
   sum of positive excess), the circuit is infeasible -> "impossible".
6. Otherwise let F0 be the flow currently on the T->S completion edge —
   that is *a* feasible S-T current value. To find the *minimum* one,
   block the T->S edge (set its remaining capacity to 0, since it must not
   be used as a free shortcut) and run one more max-flow, this time from T
   to S, over the residual graph left after step 5; call this F1. The
   units of F1 are alternate real paths that let us "cancel" some of the
   forced T->S completion flow. The answer is F0 - F1.

This matches the technique posted on the problem's own discuss board
(message 357718: "任意解 减去 反向余量图的最大流", i.e. "a feasible solution
minus the max-flow of the reverse residual graph").

## Verification

- Reproduced both sample cases exactly (answer 9, then "impossible").
- Wrote an independent brute-force oracle using `scipy.optimize.linprog`
  (LP: minimize flow leaving S subject to per-junction flow conservation
  and each edge's flow >= its required lower bound, no upper bound) and
  ran 400 random small DAG instances (N<=5, M<=8) through both the flow
  solution and the LP; zero mismatches. The LP is derived directly from
  the statement's conservation rule, independent of the flow
  implementation, so it does not share a misreading with the solver.
- Mutation testing: removing the "block the T->S edge before the second
  max-flow" step, or dropping the negative half of the excess accounting
  (`excess[p] -= cur`), both changed the sample's answer (garbage number,
  or turned case 1 into "impossible") and both were caught by the LP
  differential test (6/200 mismatches for the excess mutant). Confirms
  the sample and the oracle both have teeth.
- Stress/fuzz: compiled with `-fsanitize=address,undefined` and ran 500
  randomized datasets (up to N=50, M=200 each, including self-loop edges
  and duplicate (p,n) pairs that violate the stated uniqueness
  constraint) through the sanitized binary — no sanitizer report, no
  crash, ~0.3s total for 46000+ input lines. Also ran a single N=50,M=200
  dataset for timing: negligible (well under the 1000ms limit; the graph
  has at most ~254 nodes and ~650 edges after adding the super
  source/sink and completion edge, trivial for Dinic).
- The board (message 356596 among others) documents that this problem's
  test data is weak — an empty `int main(){}` gets Accepted — so passing
  the sample was treated as very weak evidence and the LP oracle was the
  real check.

Language: no `long long` needed anywhere (max excess sum is
M*max_current = 200*100 = 20000, fits comfortably in a 32-bit int), so
language 4 (C++) and language 0 (G++) are both fine on the arithmetic; no
board folklore for this problem favored one over the other.
