# 1714 — The Cave

## Statement and primary solution

The cubic graph consists of an inner tree whose leaves are the outer chambers, plus a cycle connecting those leaves in their planar order. Find a minimum-weight Hamiltonian cycle, with weights zero or one, and print its vertices starting at chamber 1 without repeating the final 1. Outer labels are not their cyclic order; recover that order from actual outer edges. There is one input instance, n <= 500, 1000 ms, 10000 KB.

Delete outer chamber 1 and root the remaining inner tree at its former inner neighbor. This is a binary tree. Each subtree forms an interval of outer leaves and has only three boundary vertices: its root and its first and last leaves. Store minimum-cost acyclic path forests indexed by the degrees (0..2) and connectivity partition of those boundary vertices. Leaves have a single isolated boundary vertex. Combine children by enumerating the three new edges (two tree edges and the edge joining consecutive boundary leaves). Every forgotten vertex must have degree two; every connected component must still meet the boundary; reject premature cycles. Finally reinsert chamber 1 with its three incident edges and choose a connected degree-two graph of minimum cost. Store selected-edge bitsets to recover the cycle. All costs fit in int. The state space per boundary is constant.

The discussion board's 2020 message confirms the inner-tree/binary-tree observation but supplies no code. The 2007 message only asks about search time limits. There was no compiler or input-framing correction to incorporate.

## Independent control

A separate generic small-treewidth Hamiltonian-cycle solver was written without the cave-specific root/leaf recursion. Minimum-degree elimination creates separator bags; each original edge starts a two-choice factor, and factors are bucketed by the first eliminated vertex in their scope. Bucket products sum boundary degrees and merge connectivity partitions; a premature cycle is rejected. Forget a vertex only with degree two and with its component still reaching the separator. A completed connected degree-two graph spanning all n vertices is accepted as a candidate. Factors keep their minimum cost and edge witness for each state. This is independent graph elimination, not a rewritten version of the primary subtree transition. Its complete submitted source is preserved below.

An additional unsubmitted Hamiltonian DFS control passed 500 small brute-force tests but was too slow on an original test; it was discarded and is not part of the judge-failure evidence.
