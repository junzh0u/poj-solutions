# POJ 3397 — Click the Lines

The statement asks for the minimum number of arbitrary planar click positions that select all input infinite lines. A click selects a line when its perpendicular distance is at most the positive threshold D. There are at most 200 lines and every line intersects at least one other line. The input begins with an explicit test-case count.

The second official sample contradicts this definition. It has D=1 and the lines y=0, x=0, and x+y+2=0, but prints 2. Clicking (-1,-1) has distances 1, 1, and 0 respectively, so the stated non-strict rule gives an answer of 1. This is not merely a boundary issue: clicking (-0.75,-0.75) has distances 0.75, 0.75, and 0.5/sqrt(2), all strictly less than 1. Thus changing <= to < does not reconcile the sample.

Restricting clicks to intersections of the original lines would produce the sample answer 2. Each of the three intersections misses the remaining line by more than D. However, the statement contains no such restriction, and replacing arbitrary clicks by original-line intersections is mathematically invalid for the given sample itself. A solver limited to those intersections would therefore implement a different problem. Even that version is a finite minimum set-cover problem; greedily choosing the intersection covering the most uncovered lines does not establish minimality.
