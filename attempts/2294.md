# 2294 Unfoldung

The surface consists of unit square faces of a solid assembled from face-adjacent unit cubes. Each surface edge belongs to exactly two surface faces. Input gives a leading test count, then the number of faces f (6 through 10000), an edge count n, and n strings x+y or x-y. A plus cuts the common edge; a minus preserves it. Overlapping squares in the final layout are allowed. If the uncut face graph is disconnected, DISCONNECTED takes precedence over every unfolding obstruction. Otherwise the answer is CAN UNFOLD or CANNOT UNFOLD. Limits are 1000 ms and 65536 K.

The unresolved difficulty is recovering enough geometry from adjacency alone. There are no coordinates, cyclic orders of the four edges on each face, or explicit vertex incidences. The full graph, including cut edges, is information that both attempted algorithms discarded. A stronger retry should investigate reconstructing a combinatorial embedding from that graph, then developing the squares across uncut edges and checking the consistency of every constraint. A planar embedding may help for genus-zero surfaces, but the statement does not promise genus zero: solids assembled from cubes can have tunnels. It is not established that an arbitrary embedding suffices or that the supplied graph determines the necessary embedding.

Two distinct graph-only hypotheses were submitted under C++ (language 4), and both failed:

- Run 25225298, Wrong Answer, 1461 B, 2026-10-16 05:58:25 POJ time. Union-find tracked connected components and detected cycles among uncut adjacencies. It printed DISCONNECTED first, otherwise CANNOT UNFOLD for any cycle and CAN UNFOLD for a tree. This is too restrictive: a 2 by 2 flat patch of unit faces has a four-cycle that need not be cut. A larger cubical solid can contain that patch in a valid unfolded net.
- Run 25225301, Wrong Answer, 1729 B, 2026-10-16 05:59:54 POJ time. Breadth-first coloring checked all uncut components, printed DISCONNECTED if there was more than one, otherwise CANNOT UNFOLD on a same-color edge and CAN UNFOLD if bipartite. A checkerboard coloring is necessary for a development into unit squares, but is not sufficient. For example, preserve a belt of the four lateral faces of one cube, attach the top to one lateral face and the bottom to another with one hinge each, and cut all other edges. The uncut graph is connected and bipartite but the closed belt cannot be developed with every interior angle equal to 180 degrees. Its opposite hinges on each face do not have the geometry of a 2 by 2 flat patch despite the even cycle.

The official sample has two cases: a ten-face surface with a tree of nine uncut adjacencies, whose answer is CAN UNFOLD, and a six-face cube with all twelve edges uncut, whose answer is CANNOT UNFOLD. Both attempted algorithms pass it. Deleting the cycle/conflict test changes the second answer, so the sample checks that an obstruction can exist but does not distinguish either insufficient graph criterion from the actual geometry.

Both annotated sources compiled cleanly with g++ -std=c++98 -pedantic -Wall -Wextra -O2 and reproduced the sample exactly. The first algorithm was exhaustively checked on all 4096 subsets of the twelve cube adjacencies against an independent BFS component count and edge-count oracle. The second was exhaustively checked on the same 4096 subsets against reachability and all 64 possible binary face colorings. Both passed a 10000-face path stress case. Those tests establish the implementations of the two graph criteria; they do not establish geometric correctness.
