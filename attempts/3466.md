# 3466 Distance to the Prey

No submission was made. The solve stopped at deriving an efficient exact shortest-path algorithm for the overlapping recursive web. The suggested park classification is model, not judge: no judge failure was observed and no evidence suggests broken data.

The statement gives multiple cases to EOF, each with four integer coordinates between zero and 10^9. The web is S_10000. The time limit is 1000 ms and the memory limit is 131072 KB. The sample is `5 3 6 4`, with answer `2`.

The statement's first image is essential. S_0 is the horizontal unit edge from (0,0) to (1,0). If S_n has horizontal extent L=3^n, S_(n+1) consists of five copies, in these coordinates, where (x,y) ranges over S_n:

- (x,y)
- (L-y,x)
- (L+x,L+y)
- (2L+y,L-x)
- (2L+x,y)

Thus the path substitution is a horizontal edge, an upward edge, a horizontal edge, a downward edge, and a horizontal edge, with each edge replaced recursively and with rotations preserving the construction. The bounding box of S_n is [0,3^n] by [0,(3^n-1)/2]. The origin is the left endpoint at the bottom. The sample's hint confirms these coordinates: (6,4) can move to (6,3), then to (5,3).

The critical obstacle is that rotated neighboring copies meet away from their connecting endpoints. The web is the union of geometric edges, and the spider can switch branches at a shared vertex. It is not valid simply to assign each point an index along the recursively drawn curve and subtract indices.

Explicitly generating all edges for small orders established the following intersections. Let the five copies in S_(n+1) be numbered zero through four and let L=3^n. Copies zero and one share every integer point (L-t,t), for 0 <= t <= (L-1)/2. Copies three and four share every point (2L+t,t) over the same range. Copies one and two share only (L,L), and copies two and three share only (2L,L). No other pair intersects in the generated orders. This was checked through S_6, where the first intersection has 122 vertices. These observations need a proof before being used as a recurrence.

For example, S_2's first and second copies share (3,0) and (2,1). S_3's first and second copies share (9,0), (8,1), (7,2), (6,3), and (5,4). Treating every copy as connected to its neighbors only at its nominal endpoints therefore loses shortcuts. At the input bound, enumerating all shared vertices is also infeasible.
