# POJ 3730 — Ebbinghaus method

No submissions were made in this fresh gpt-6.1-sol attempt. The obstacle is the algorithm, not submission infrastructure or evidence of a broken judge. Suggest a model park for a stronger-model retry.

The statement gives n wordlists and m increasing review days a[i], with n,m <= 1000, a[1] <= 100, and successive review-day differences <= 100. Starting a wordlist on calendar day s consumes 80 minutes on s and 40 minutes on every s+a[i]-1. Each calendar day permits 120 minutes. At most one wordlist can start each day. Lists must start in order, but they otherwise have identical requirements. Minimize the last required review day, which is the final starting day plus a[m]-1. The sample n=5, a=(2,4,8,15,30) has answer 36.

An exact formulation assigns an increasing sequence of start days s[1],...,s[n], with s[1]=1 without loss of generality. In 40-minute units the daily load is twice the indicator that a list starts that day plus the number of scheduled reviews that day. Every load must be at most three. The shifted review pattern of each new list must fit all future daily capacities, not merely the capacity on its starting day. A review on a[1]=1 is permitted by the bounds and would consume the remaining unit on the starting day.

The tempting algorithm scans days in ascending order, starts the next list whenever the entire shifted pattern fits, and reserves its review slots. This is feasible but is not optimal. The discussion thread http://poj.org/showmessage?message_id=118200 supplies the separating input:

```
4 4
2 3 4 6
```

The greedy starts are 1,2,5,8, finishing on day 13. The optimum starts are 1,3,5,7, finishing on day 12. Every daily load of the latter is at most three. Enumerating every increasing four-start schedule with first start 1 and final start at most 8 confirms that no final start below 7 is feasible. The sample does not catch this error: greedy and exact enumeration both finish on day 36 with starts 1,2,3,5,7.

The independent small reference enumerated start-day subsets and rebuilt the complete daily workload for each schedule. Its positive control was the board case above. For n=4, all 56 choices of one through four distinct review days drawn from 2 through 7 were exhaustively tested; greedy was suboptimal on 14 patterns. Thus this is a recurrent algorithmic defect, not an isolated indexing error. The reference is exponential in the number of starts and does not scale to n=1000.
