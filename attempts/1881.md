POJ 1881, Sail Race, was attempted with a direct geometric simulation and received Wrong Answer once. The statement asks for minimum-distance routes with the minimum number of tacks on each leg. Outside the forbidden wind cone, travel directly and select the point, reach, or downwind speed ratio using the smallest angle off the wind. Inside the cone, use exactly two courses at wind direction plus and minus the point angle.

For a leg of length L at signed angle delta off the wind and point angle p, let F = L cos(delta) and S = L sin(delta). The distances on the clockwise and counterclockwise cone boundaries are (F/cos(p) + S/sin(p))/2 and (F/cos(p) - S/sin(p))/2. Print the clockwise boundary first. The total layout length is the sum of direct leg lengths; the sailed length sums all tack distances. Sailing time sums distance divided by speed. With T printed tack segments, tack penalty is (T-1) times the input penalty, matching the published sample's five penalties for six segments.

Submission 25225473 used language C++, 2186 bytes, and received Wrong Answer. It normalized compass bearings into [0,360), used atan2(dx,dy), used a 1e-9 tolerance at speed-band boundaries, printed one decimal for bearings and speed and two for distances and times, and printed blank lines after the header, each leg, and the summary. Its numeric sample results matched the statement, including total 64.34 nautical miles and duration 11.47 hours with 0.50 penalty. The tack lines omitted the nm suffix according to the discussion correction.

The POJ discussion at showmessage?message_id=116567 explicitly says to remove the nm suffix on every tack-distance line. It supplies a corrected sample whose duration is 11.67 rather than 11.47. That latter correction contradicts direct arithmetic: 20/5 + sqrt(109)/5 + sqrt(314)/6.7 + sqrt(125)/7.5 + 5/6.7 + 0.5 is approximately 11.4685. Thus 11.47 was retained, and the discussion's duration correction was not blindly adopted.

The original 1995 World Finals problem D statement is available at https://icpc.global/worldfinals/problems/1995-ICPC-World-Finals/1995WorldFinalProblemSet.pdf. It confirms 11.47, but originally allows unspecified labeled output formatting. POJ replaced that convention with exact formatting. No official judge data was found in a short targeted search. The drawing was inspected and agrees with the angle convention.

Two other discussion messages, 7421 and 7529, point out that the sample's first leg could equally start north instead of east and ask whether a special judge is needed. Neither provides a resolution. The statement gives no ordering rule for the two equally valid tacks. The attempted implementation follows the sample's clockwise-boundary-first convention, but the judge could use another convention, particularly around north or when the target lies on the other side of the wind. This remains an unresolved hypothesis rather than evidence of a judge defect.

Local verification compiled cleanly as C++98 with warnings enabled. Ten thousand randomized cone cases certified that both segment lengths are nonnegative and their actual compass displacement reconstructs the target to below 1e-7. The sample cannot detect swapping the two unequal-distance formulas because its only tacking leg has equal distances. Independent reconstruction detects that mutation on asymmetric cases. These checks validate the geometry but cannot validate an undocumented tack ordering or POJ's exact output oracle.
