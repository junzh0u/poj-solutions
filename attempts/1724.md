# 1724 ROADS — not accepted

Five submissions, all Wrong Answer. Parked because the evidence points at the judge rather than the solution, not because the approach ran out of road.

## The problem

Cheapest-by-length route from city 1 to city N under a coin budget K. Directed roads, each with a length and a toll.

## What was tried

Budget-constrained shortest path over states of `(city, coins spent)` rather than city alone — a longer route that costs less can beat a shorter expensive one, so a single-state Dijkstra settles the wrong thing. Edge lengths are positive, so an optimal route never repeats a city and total length is bounded by `(N-1) * maxLen`; states are bucketed by exact distance (Dial's, no heap) and a `(city, cost)` state is expanded only when it strictly beats the cheapest cost already recorded for that city. The destination's first dequeue at cost `<= K` is the answer.

An earlier priority-queue version hit 1.3s on an adversarial case; the bucket queue brought that to ~0.12s.
