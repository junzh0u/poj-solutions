The statement gives up to 200 unit squares and 300 prescribed hinges. Directions 0, 1, 2, 3 mean up, left, down, right in the original plane. Fold values 0, 1, 2 mean flat, forward, backward. Unmentioned adjacent sides may separate. The target is a finite cell boundary, possibly with disconnected interior, with no coincident squares and no interpenetration at required hinges. POJ specifies repeated n/e records terminated by 0 0 and output `Test case #k: closed surface, volume=v` or `Test case #k: not a closed surface`.

The attempted algorithm propagates an integer frame for each square through the input graph. Coordinates are doubled. The initial center is (0,0,0), its right/up basis is (1,0,0)/(0,1,0), and its normal is (0,0,1). For a neighbor in direction d, a flat hinge has neighbor center c+2d and unchanged basis. A forward hinge has center c+d+n and rotates d to n and n to -d, preserving the hinge axis. A backward hinge has center c+d-n and rotates d to -n and n to d. Reverse graph arcs use the opposite planar direction and the same fold value. Already visited frames must agree exactly. Reversing every fold reflects the solid and should preserve both validity and volume.

For each placed square, normalize its normal to the positive coordinate direction and reject duplicate (center, normal) pairs. Its four doubled-coordinate corners define four undirected unit grid edges. Every such edge must have even incidence. With unique squares, this is the mod-2 closure test. If a prescribed flat hinge lies on an edge with four incident squares, reject it: the other pair crosses the connected flat sheet. For every x-normal square, group its x coordinate by its (y,z) center. Sort the crossings on each ray, require an even count, and add alternating differences divided by two to obtain volume. The implementation uses C++98 vectors, maps, sets, and 32-bit coordinates and volume.

The new implementation propagates doubled-coordinate square frames from center (1,1,0), right (1,0,0), up (0,1,0), normal (0,0,1), using the same geometric transformations described above. It rejects inconsistent cycles and duplicate faces. Each square's four corner coordinates are even, so division by two gives integer grid vertices exactly, including negative coordinates. It constructs the smallest axis-aligned cell box containing all faces plus one layer of exterior cells. For a face normal to axis k, the lower adjacent cell has coordinate (center[k]-2)/2 along k and (center[j]-1)/2 along each other axis. A bit on that cell marks the face separating it from its positive-k neighbor. BFS assigns every cell a parity label: crossing a marked face toggles inside/outside, and crossing an unmarked adjacency preserves it. The padded corner is outside. Any conflicting label rejects the surface; otherwise the count of inside cells is the volume. Thus each square is explicitly checked as a separator of different labels, and holes are detected as inconsistent paths. The flat-hinge/four-faces rejection remains.
