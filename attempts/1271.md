## Reading of the problem

The bread is a convex polygon (n vertices, 3<=n<=20, given CCW). Resting it on one
side puts that side at the "bottom", and milk fills to depth h measured from that
bottom, covering the strip of the polygon within perpendicular distance h of that
side. The statement never spells out what "at most k actions" buys you, and the
natural first reading -- physically roll the bread over an adjacent edge, one roll
per action, keep only the final orientation's coverage -- fails both the sample and
a board-posted stress case, so it is not the intended reading.

The reading that reproduces both known-good outputs: Tomy may dip the bread on up
to k of its n sides, chosen freely with no adjacency requirement between dips, and
milk already soaked in never dries, so the final wet area is the UNION, over the
chosen sides, of the h-strip for each side. He wants the subset of size <= k that
maximizes this union area.

By De Morgan, the complement of that union is the INTERSECTION of the "still dry"
half-planes (distance > h from every chosen side), and intersecting more
half-planes can only shrink or preserve the intersection -- so the union is
maximized, and the dry area minimized, by always using the full budget
m = min(k, n) sides. The answer is total polygon area minus the minimum, over all
C(n, m) subsets of sides, of the area left after clipping the polygon by that
subset's m "still dry" half-planes.

Verified this reading two ways before trusting it:
- Reproduces the sample exactly: n=4,k=2,h=1 on vertices (1,0),(3,0),(5,2),(0,4)
  gives 7.46 (the naive single-orientation reading tops out at 4.81, the "any
  contiguous roll window" reading tops out at 9.41 for this case -- neither
  matches; only the free-subset-union reading gives exactly 7.46, achieved by
  the pair of edges (2,3)).
- Reproduces a board-posted stress case exactly: n=20,k=8,h=1 on the parabola
  points (0,0),(1,1),(2,4),...,(19,361) (poj.org/showmessage?message_id=160734)
  gives 635.55, matching the poster's own answer.
- Cross-checked against an independent Monte Carlo point-sampling estimate of the
  true union area (not reusing the half-plane-clip code) on 30 random small
  convex polygons (n from 3 to 9, k from 0 to 8, h integer 0-10); every case
  agreed with the closed-form/DFS answer within Monte Carlo sampling noise.
- h=0 or k=0 forcing output 0 (rather than the polygon's total area) falls out of
  the formula automatically and needs no special case -- the board's own solved-it
  writeup (message_id=139503) flags this as the standard bug when the dry area is
  computed the wrong way around.

## Algorithm and implementation

For each side i (vertex i to vertex i+1, CCW so the interior sits at local y>=0 in
that side's own frame), precompute the "dry" half-plane local y>=h directly in
original coordinates (no per-subset trig). Search over subsets of size m=min(k,n)
via a depth-bounded (depth<=k<=8) DFS over strictly increasing side indices,
clipping incrementally (each node shares its parent's already-clipped polygon
rather than re-clipping from scratch at every leaf -- this cuts the total work from
C(n,m)*m clips down to roughly sum_{i=0..m} C(n,i) nodes, each one clip), and
aborting the whole search the instant a clip empties the polygon since 0 is the
best possible dry area. This made an from-scratch-per-subset brute force (which
independently confirmed the same answers on the sample, board case, and 30 random
cases) run in ~1.7s for a worst-case n=20,k=8 x10 test cases into ~0.1s optimized /
~0.4s at -O0, comfortably inside the 1000ms limit even accounting for a slow judge
machine and even unoptimized. Verified with ASan/UBSan across the sample, board
case, 30 random cases, and 60 more n=20/random-k/random-h stress cases -- no
memory or UB issues found.
