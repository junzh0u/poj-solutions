## Statement and approach

There are at most 50 infinite bidirectional pathway lines. Jogging on the ground has speed v2, and jogging on a pathway has speed v1+v2. Every boarding costs that line's Ti+, every leaving costs Ti-, and changing lines at their intersection costs the sum. Start and finish are not on a line. Output the minimum time and a route of at most 300 labelled segments, with six decimal places. The two input points define an infinite line, not a finite segment.

An optimal route can be chosen with ground travel only at its beginning and end. For an intermediate ground segment connecting nonparallel pathways, scale its two endpoints toward the lines' intersection. Until an adjacent ride vanishes, the ride lengths and the ground-segment length are linear in the scale parameter, so one endpoint of the feasible interval is no worse. This either removes the ground segment or removes a ride; in the latter case, join the neighboring ground segments and avoid the unnecessary boarding/leaving costs. For parallel pathways, translate the connecting segment along the common direction until an adjacent ride vanishes. Repeating gives a chain of pathways connected at intersections.

For a point P and a line with unit direction d, let Q be P's orthogonal projection and h its distance from the line. The two tangent entry/exit candidates are Q +/- d*h*v2/sqrt((v1+v2)^2-v2^2). Create these candidates for both start and finish on each line, plus a separate state on each participating line at every nonparallel intersection. Connect consecutive states along each line at the combined speed; connect the two intersection states with directional leaving-plus-boarding costs. Start edges include boarding and final edges include leaving. Add the direct ground route. Dijkstra finds the optimum, and merging consecutive segments on the same pathway keeps the witness short. Zero-length ground segments represent transfers at intersections.

## Verification

The annotated C++98 implementation reproduced the official sample byte for byte after normalizing negative zero. It agreed in objective value with the independent historical Accepted program on 350 seeded random instances with zero to eight pathways. A separate output checker verified segment counts and labels, that every pathway segment lies on its claimed line, the final endpoint, and the total recomputed from Euclidean lengths and boarding/leaving charges. The checker rejected deliberately corrupted cost and off-line witnesses. Additional cases covered parallel lines, coincident start and finish with no pathways, and zero-cost crossing transfers. A 50-line case ran in about 0.003 seconds locally.

The sample does not test nonzero boarding/leaving costs: a mutant omitting these costs still passes it. The following separating case makes the direct ground route optimal, with time 20.001000 and one segment to (100,1): one pathway; start (-100,-1), finish (100,1), v1=90, v2=10; pathway through (0,0),(1,0), boarding=10, leaving=10. The charge-omitting mutant takes the pathway and is rejected by this case.

POJ discussion message 350691 questions the no-intermediate-ground property; the scaling argument above resolves that concern. Message 10489 confirms that pathways are infinite. Message 165498 contains a complete independent source by JiaJunpeng with a historical Accepted header (5276K, 3125MS, C++), dated 2011-10-14. Its construction solves tangent equations between pairs of pathways and connects states through a dense Dijkstra search. This program was compiled locally, reproduced the sample, and served as the independent reference. For the control submission it was retained unchanged apart from a leading identification and attribution comment block and normalized line endings.
